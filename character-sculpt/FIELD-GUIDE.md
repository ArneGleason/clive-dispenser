# Clive sculpt relay field guide

Use this page to choose an approach, avoid repeating a failed experiment, and leave a result the next sculptor can test. The [approved turnaround](references/clive-turnaround.png) is the visual target. The [gallery](gallery/README.md) shows the saved candidates, the [experiment log](LOG.md) links to each run, and [Issue #3](https://github.com/ArneGleason/clive-dispenser/issues/3) is open for sketches, hypotheses, and critiques. Anyone taking a turn can update this guide.

## What has worked, and when

| When you see this | What helped in Clive | Limits and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| The figure resembles a generic gnome, or the proportions drift as you orbit. | Check broad landmarks against the approved front, side, and back before detailing. The reference gave the subdivision study a better lean. Neon’s later head and hand realignment made a larger difference than adding sculpt detail alone. | The first text-only sculpt missed the likeness. The three reference views disagree in places. Record which reading you chose and check it on the same 3D form from other angles. [Text-only baseline](../experiments/2026-11-21-blender-sculpt/README.md) · [Turn 4 pose study](../experiments/2026-11-21-turnaround-pose-face/README.md) |
| The wink, cheek, and grin fall apart from below. | Use a continuous face cage to carry the cheek into the lid. Keep eye relief shallow and check it in unpainted clay. | Inset cheek patches pinched at their boundaries, a Gaussian nose push made a beak in profile, and a raised catchlight read as a pellet in clay. A continuous quad surface, rounded nose volume, and shallow iris relief held up better. [Matched face close-ups](../experiments/2026-11-21-turnaround-pose-face/Comparison-Face-Clay.jpg) · [Revisions and failures](../experiments/2026-11-21-turnaround-pose-face/README.md) |
| Separate parts read as a kit and hide the garment’s anatomy. | A fused signed-distance field gave turn 5 continuous seams. Modeling a waist, fly, seat and two legs made the lowered trousers recognizable. | This tested hand-built garment volumes. How cloth compresses under the hands remains an open question. The fused turn lost the editability of a Blender cage and weakened the profile and expression. Use the raw rebuild for mesh evaluation. The decimated blend has boundary and non-manifold edges. [Turn 5 contact renders](../experiments/2026-11-21-fused-clay-sdf/Comparison-Contact-Clay.jpg) · [Mesh findings](../experiments/2026-11-21-fused-clay-sdf/README.md) |
| The cap looks stacked, or the hair looks like tubes. | Author the main curves in 3D. Vary their cross-sections according to material. Turn 6 put broad felt folds into a bending cap, fused grooved hair ribbons over a backing, and gave the boots a firm welt and narrow flex creases. | The initial cap sweep pinched under the hook. Separate ribbons still read as parts, and tucking only the hair left the ear exposed through the cap. Test the junctions in high rear and overhead clay before painting. [Matched details](../experiments/2026-11-21-felt-and-carved-hair/Comparison-Details.jpg) · [Cap iterations](../experiments/2026-11-21-felt-and-carved-hair/Iterations.jpg) |
| The shirt floats off the seat, or the seat seems separate from the legs. | Build the supporting anatomy, then fit the fabric to it. Turn 7 used one quad pelvis branching into two thighs and fitted the hem and waistband to that surface. | Retaining the old hem placement cut a notch where the skin poked through. The first single-loop skin loft exposed a patch below the band. The fitted shirt improved, but the seat became pointed in profile. [Same-seat hem test](../experiments/2026-11-21-seat-and-smock/Fit-Test.jpg) · [Matched side and low rear](../experiments/2026-11-21-seat-and-smock/Comparison-Clay.jpg) |
| A volume comes to a point while the overall pose is working. | Compare retreat above and below the widest point. Turn 8 restored fullness around the seat’s apex at nearly the same rear projection and refitted the hem and band to the revised surface. | Shaving the apex shortened the projection and left a shoulder. The broader arc matched the approved local profile more closely. Low rear still shows the waistband as a separate roll, and the 2D trace cannot settle how the shape should look from every angle. [Reference and two trials](../experiments/2026-11-21-rounded-seat/Reference-Profile.jpg) · [Matched clay](../experiments/2026-11-21-rounded-seat/Comparison-Clay.jpg) |
| The waistband reads separately from the legs, rims look knife-sharp, arms/hands read tubular, or hair shows a plain backing egg from behind. | Build one forked two-leg trouser cage compressed by hand-surface `BVHTree` proximity over monotonic C1 semicircular hems; compute beard lock groove normals radially from a 3D spine with flank/rear locks burying the core `2.4+ mm`; voxel-fuse forearm–wrist–palm–fingers per side; flatten boot flanks with superellipses (`p > 2.2`) and define the welt/heel step. | Uncapped thigh-center raycasts crossing `x = 0` hit the *opposite* thigh and teleport crotch bridge vertices into spikes (guard `abs(p.x) > 1.8` and `hit.x * center.x >= 0.5`). Steep narrow trouser fold bells (`Δr = 2.45 mm` on `22` rings) form thin horizontal fins rather than chunky rope folds; reversing `z_off` in a rim roll creates an unintended shelf; separate sleeves still leave an armhole seam in `rear_high`. ## How to make a comparison another agent can use

1. Name the parent, the specific feature, and the angle where you expect it to improve. Choose any saved Clive or start fresh.
2. Review matched neutral clay first. Check the front, side, and two oblique views, including a view likely to expose a failure. Use color to test glaze and material readability. [Review renderer and workflow](WORKFLOW.md) · [Turn 8 color pair](../experiments/2026-11-21-rounded-seat/Comparison-Color.jpg)
3. Keep three judgments visible in the run record. What looked better, what the mesh checks showed, and what someone actually printed. The current candidates have local geometry checks. A physical print has yet to be recorded. The original dispenser is the separate mechanical baseline. Its 25 virtual checks cover assembly and motion, with friction and tolerances still to test in hardware. [Dispenser report](../report.json) · [Mechanical test notes](../README.md#the-virtual-test)

Look for tension lines from the fingers and chunky folds above the boots. Thin, papery folds would miss the ceramic character. ## Add or revise a finding

When a turn teaches something another sculptor can use, add or update a row above. Say **when to try it**, **what happened in which view**, and **where it broke or where the evidence ends**. Link a matched image or run record and credit the earlier work. If a later experiment overturns a finding, link both results and describe what changed. Bring open or conflicting ideas to [Issue #3](https://github.com/ArneGleason/clive-dispenser/issues/3).
