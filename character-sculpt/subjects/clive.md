## Shape language · owner guidance, 2026-11-21

After five turns Arne's main critique applies to every model so far: the parts are in roughly the right places, but they are drawn without character. A rim that is a ring. Folds that are stripes. Locks that run straight down one axis. The appeal of the sheet is in its curves: where they dip and lift, how their weight changes, and how one line leads into the next.

**Read the sheet as art direction, not as a blueprint.** The three views were image-generated, each composed to look good from its own angle. They almost certainly do not describe one consistent solid. Where they disagree, do not average them and do not copy one view literally. Work out what the design is doing (which curve, which rhythm, which material) and build one three-dimensional form that delivers it from every angle. That means bringing your own consistent set of shape rules and applying them over the whole figure.

| Element | What the sheet does | What it should feel like |
|---|---|---|
| Cap rim | It is not a ring round the head. From the front it arches over the brow and sweeps down over both temples. From the side it runs from high on the forehead down behind the ear to the nape, then kicks up a little. From the back it droops in a soft scallop. The edge is thick and flares like a bell; it is not a rolled tube. It conforms to the head and at the same time tells you the head's shape. | Worn felt: heavy, soft, slumped. |
| Cap body | Three or four broad folds spiral diagonally up from the rim and converge into the hook, narrowing as they climb. Ridges are round and wide, valleys tighter. The crown slumps back under its own weight before the hook curls forward. | The same felt. |
| Beard and moustache | Locks start around the mouth and travel in long S-curves: outward, down, then the whole beard swings to his right at the tip. In profile it pushes forward off the chin, drops, and swings back toward the chest. The curve lives in all three dimensions. Each lock swells and tapers, ends curl, and the outer edge is scalloped at staggered heights. | Stylised, sculptural hair. |
| Hair | Overlapping comma- and S-shaped locks in tiers. Each lock is a ribbon with two or three engraved grooves and an end rolled into a small volute, with crisp valleys between locks. Arne's comparison is the carved hair on ancient Greek and Roman statues, not strands and not wool. | Carved, not grown. |
| Skin | Cheeks, nose, knuckles and seat are plump convex forms that flow into each other with no hard edges. | One coherent, rounded, organic surface. |
| Smock and trousers | The trousers are thick rope-like rolls that sag between the legs and twist as they go round. The smock is pulled taut over the back with a few long folds running to the hem. | Cloth under tension and gravity. |
| Boots | Bulbous toe cap, projecting welt and sole, a heel block, a few deep buckled creases at ankle and vamp, and a shaft that slouches in one broad fold. | Stiff leather: firm planes and sharp creases, not soft blobs. |

Rules to hold across the whole figure:

1. **Every edge, fold and lock is a designed curve in three dimensions.** Check it from the front, the side and above. If it projects to a straight line or a plain circle in any of those views, it has probably been placed rather than designed.
2. **Curves carry weight.** Folds and locks swell and taper. Avoid constant width and even spacing; look for big, medium and small.
3. **Lines lead into each other.** The cap folds lead the eye to the hook. The beard continues the sweep of the moustache. The rim's curve carries on into the hair.
4. **Material decides the fold.** Felt makes a few fat, soft folds and sags. Leather makes sharp creases where it flexes and stays flat between them. Cloth makes tension lines from wherever it is pulled and bunches where it is released. Skin only creases where it is squeezed. Hair is grouped, grooved and crisp.
5. **Asymmetry is intended.** The hook leans one way, the beard swings the other, the grin is crooked.
